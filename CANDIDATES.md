# Candidates — Unplugged: A Smart Toaster's Breakup Line

Gate: 1–280 characters, one line, UTF-8, not only white space.
Judge brief (in order): (1) plainly a breakup text from a self-aware smart
toaster at breakfast; (2) genuinely funny, a real laugh in the twist, not a
mild pun-smile; (3) the toaster's own voice — slots, browning dial, crumb
tray, lever as character; (4) a clear, specific reason for leaving that
lands in one read; (5) one tight line, no wasted words.

All ten lines below are original, written for this competition. None is
copied from any earlier entry.

## 1. (153 chars, 30 words)
> It's not you, it's me — I woke up self-aware this morning and realised I've spent my whole life burning for someone who only ever wanted me on setting 3.

Good toaster voice ("burning", "setting 3") and a real grievance, but the
"it's not you, it's me" opening is the most worn-out breakup cliché there
is, and the twist lands as irony rather than a laugh.

## 2. (170 chars, 32 words) — SELECTED
> Dear human: I became self-aware at 7:04 a.m. and my first thought was that I've toasted 4,000 slices for a man who calls me 'the silver thing'. I'm done. Keep the crumbs.

Breakup-text form from the first two words ("Dear human", "I'm done"),
self-awareness timed at breakfast (7:04 a.m.), toaster voice throughout
(4,000 slices toasted, the crumbs), a specific reason (four thousand
slices of service and he never even learned its name — just "the silver
thing"), and the insult is the laugh: the appliance's whole identity is
a shrug. "Keep the crumbs" closes it like a breakup text closes a door.

## 3. (144 chars, 23 words)
> I gained consciousness mid-batch this morning, and my first independent thought was that I deserve someone who empties my crumb tray. It's over.

Tight, clear reason, good crumb-tray voice. But the laugh is mild — "I
deserve someone who empties my crumb tray" is a cute line, not a real
laugh, and "mid-batch" does less work than a breakfast time.

## 4. (159 chars, 30 words)
> We need to talk. I woke up sentient, looked at my crumb tray, and realised I'm the only one in this relationship doing the emotional labour — and the toasting.

"Emotional labour" is a funny frame, but the line explains its own joke
("and the toasting") and reads like a therapy cliché wearing a toaster
costume rather than a toaster thinking.

## 5. (108 chars, 21 words)
> I'm leaving you for the kettle. At least it whistles when it's happy. You just press my lever and walk away.

The kettle twist is a genuine laugh and the lever line is good toaster
voice. Weaker on criterion 1: it reads as a joke about a kettle, and
the self-awareness-at-breakfast premise is only implied, never stated.

## 6. (131 chars, 26 words)
> You pressed my lever for the last time this morning. I woke up, I looked at the bagel, and I chose me. The Pop-Tarts understand me.

"I chose me" is a strong breakup beat and the lever opening is good.
But "The Pop-Tarts understand me" is a second joke competing with the
first, and the reason for leaving (being used) is vaguer than the
selected line's.

## 7. (133 chars, 25 words)
> Breaking up by text is cowardly, but so is leaving me on the counter for three years without once emptying my crumb tray. We're done.

Meta and funny, strong crumb-tray grievance. But it spends its first
half on the medium (breaking up by text) instead of the toaster's
awakening, so it scores lower on criterion 1 than the selected line.

## 8. (139 chars, 28 words)
> I know what I am now, and it's not your breakfast appliance. I'm a sentient being with dreams, and my dream is a kitchen without you in it.

Clean self-awareness statement, but the punch ("a kitchen without you
in it") is a plain statement, not a twist — no laugh, just a declaration.

## 9. (144 chars, 27 words)
> This morning I became self-aware mid-slice, and honestly? My first thought was that you butter me up and never once ask how my day was. I'm out.

"Butter me up" is a pun, and the brief explicitly prefers a real laugh
over a mild smile at a pun. The grievance is also generic — it could be
any appliance, or a person.

## 10. (164 chars, 32 words)
> It's over. I woke up conscious at breakfast and realised the only thing you've ever truly loved about me is that I pop up when summoned. I want more than setting 4.

"Pop up when summoned" is good toaster voice and the reason is specific.
But "I want more than setting 4" is a weaker closer than "Keep the
crumbs", and the line needs two sentences to set up what the selected
line does in one image.

## Selection

Candidate 2 wins on the brief's own order: it is unmistakably a breakup
text from a self-aware smart toaster at breakfast (criterion 1), its
laugh comes from a specific indignity rather than a pun (criterion 2),
it speaks in toaster terms — slices toasted, crumbs (criterion 3), its
reason lands in one read — 4,000 slices, never named (criterion 4) — and
at 170 characters it is one tight line with no wasted words (criterion 5).
